Designs Act 2000

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

The Designs Act 2000 is a legislation in India that protects original designs, which are applied to or used in the manufacture of articles. This Act aims to promote design activity in various industries, including textiles, ceramics, and other manufactured goods, by providing a legal framework for the registration and protection of designs. The Act came into force on May 11, 2001, and has since been amended to keep pace with the changing design landscape in India.

Overview

The Designs Act 2000 provides a comprehensive framework for the protection of designs in India, defining what constitutes a design, the requirements for registration, and the rights conferred on the registered proprietor. The Act applies to all types of designs, including two-dimensional and three-dimensional designs, and covers a wide range of articles, from textiles and clothing to machinery and electrical appliances. The registration of a design under this Act gives the owner exclusive rights to apply the design to the article for which it is registered, and to prevent others from using the same design without permission. The Act also provides for the cancellation of a registered design if it is found to be not new or original [1].

Legal Framework

The Designs Act 2000 is based on the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) treaty, and is in line with international standards for design protection. The Act is divided into several sections, including Section 2, which defines key terms such as "design" and "article"; Section 4, which outlines the requirements for registration; and Section 11, which deals with the cancellation of a registered design. The Act also provides for the establishment of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks, who is responsible for administering the design registration system [2]. The Designs Rules 2001, made under the Act, provide further details on the procedure for registration and other aspects of design protection.

Procedure

To register a design under the Designs Act 2000, an application must be filed with the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks, in the prescribed form and with the required fee. The application must include a representation of the design, as well as a statement of the novelty of the design [3]. The Controller will then examine the application to determine whether the design meets the requirements for registration, including novelty, originality, and non-obviousness. If the design is found to be registrable, a certificate of registration will be issued, and the design will be protected for a period of 10 years from the date of registration.

Key Cases

  • Ramdev Food Products Pvt Ltd vs. Arvindbhai Rambhai Patel (2015, Gujarat High Court): Held that a design registration can be cancelled if the design is not new or original [4].
  • Micolube India Ltd vs. Rakesh Kumar Jain (2013, Delhi High Court): Established that the registration of a design does not give the owner the right to prevent others from using a similar design for a different article [5].
  • Carlsberg Breweries vs. Som Distilleries and Breweries (2018, Supreme Court): Decided that a design registration can be challenged on the grounds of non-compliance with the requirements of the Designs Act [6].
