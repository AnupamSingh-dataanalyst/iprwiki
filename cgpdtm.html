Controller General of Patents Designs and Trade Marks

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks is the highest authority in the Indian patent office, responsible for overseeing the administration of intellectual property rights in India. This office is responsible for the grant of patents, registration of designs, and trade marks, and also for the administration of the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [1]. The Controller General is also responsible for advising the government on matters related to intellectual property rights.

Overview

The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks is the head of the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks, which is a part of the Department of Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade, Ministry of Commerce and Industry [2]. The office is responsible for the implementation of the Patents Act, 1970, the Designs Act, 2000, the Trade Marks Act, 1999, and the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [3]. The Controller General is assisted by several Controllers and Joint Controllers who are responsible for different aspects of intellectual property rights. The office has its headquarters in Mumbai and has several branches and sub-offices across the country.

Legal Framework

The Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks derives its powers from the Patents Act, 1970, the Designs Act, 2000, the Trade Marks Act, 1999, and the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [4]. Specifically, Section 73 of the Patents Act, 1970, Section 6 of the Designs Act, 2000, Section 3 of the Trade Marks Act, 1999, and Section 3 of the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, empower the Controller General to perform various functions [5]. The Controller General is also guided by the Patent Rules, 2003, the Design Rules, 2001, the Trade Marks Rules, 2017, and the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Rules, 2002 [6].

Procedure

The procedure for appointment of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks is not explicitly stated in the statutes, but it is typically done through a selection process by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry [7]. The term of office of the Controller General is also not fixed and is typically determined by the government. The Controller General is responsible for the overall administration of the office and for making policy decisions. The office has a hierarchical structure, with the Controller General at the top, followed by Controllers and Joint Controllers, and then examiners and other support staff.
