Provisional Patent Application India

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

The provisional patent application in India is a preliminary application that allows inventors to file for patent protection without completing the full formalities of a regular patent application. This provision is particularly useful for inventors who are still in the process of developing their inventions or need time to assess the viability of their invention. By filing a provisional patent application, inventors can secure a priority date for their invention, which can be crucial in establishing the novelty of their invention.

Overview

A provisional patent application in India is a type of patent application that can be filed with the Indian Patent Office (IPO) to establish a priority date for an invention. The provisional application is a temporary application that must be followed by a complete patent application within 12 months from the date of filing of the provisional application [1]. The provisional patent application is an effective way for inventors to protect their inventions while they are still in the development stage or while they are assessing the commercial viability of their invention. The provisional patent application is also a cost-effective way to secure patent protection, as it requires less documentation and formalities compared to a complete patent application. The Indian Patent Office (IPO) provides a facility to file provisional patent applications online through its website [2].

Legal Framework

The provisional patent application in India is governed by the Patents Act, 1970, and the Patents Rules, 2003. According to Section 9 of the Patents Act, 1970, a provisional specification can be filed along with a provisional patent application [3]. The provisional specification should describe the invention and must be accompanied by the prescribed fee. Rule 13 of the Patents Rules, 2003, provides the details of the documents and information required to be filed along with a provisional patent application [4]. The Indian Patent Office (IPO) has also issued guidelines for filing provisional patent applications, which are available on its website [5].

Procedure

To file a provisional patent application in India, the applicant must follow these steps: (1) prepare a provisional specification that describes the invention, (2) prepare the necessary drawings and diagrams, (3) fill out the patent application form, (4) pay the prescribed fee, and (5) file the application with the Indian Patent Office (IPO) [6]. The applicant can file the provisional patent application online or offline. Once the provisional patent application is filed, the applicant will receive an application number and a filing date, which will be the priority date for the invention. The applicant must then file a complete patent application within 12 months from the date of filing of the provisional application.

Key Cases

  • Bishwanath Prasad Radhey Shyam vs. Hindustan Metal Industries (1978) - The court held that a provisional patent application can be filed to secure a priority date for an invention [7].
  • Novartis AG vs. Union of India (2013) - The court held that a provisional patent application must be followed by a complete patent application within 12 months [8].
  • F. Hoffmann-La Roche Ltd. vs. Cipla Ltd. (2015) - The court held that a provisional patent application can be used to establish a priority date for an invention, but it must be followed by a complete patent application [9].
