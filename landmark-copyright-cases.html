Landmark Copyright Cases in India

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

The Indian copyright regime has witnessed several landmark cases that have shaped the country's intellectual property landscape. These cases have not only clarified the interpretation of various provisions of the Copyright Act, 1957, but also established important precedents for future litigation. The judgments delivered by the Indian courts in these cases have had a significant impact on the development of copyright law in India.

Overview

The Copyright Act, 1957, is the primary legislation governing copyright law in India [1]. The Act provides for the protection of original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works, as well as cinematographic films and sound recordings. Over the years, the Indian courts have had the opportunity to interpret and apply the provisions of the Act in various landmark cases. These cases have dealt with issues such as copyright infringement, ownership, and duration, and have helped to establish the boundaries of copyright protection in India. The judgments delivered in these cases have been instrumental in shaping the country's copyright regime and have provided guidance to creators, users, and the judiciary.

Legal Framework

The Copyright Act, 1957, is based on the British Copyright Act, 1911, and has undergone several amendments since its enactment [2]. The Act is divided into 14 chapters and contains 79 sections, which provide for the protection of various types of works, including literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works. Section 2 of the Act defines the term "work" and includes within its scope original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works. Section 17 of the Act deals with the ownership of copyright, while Section 24 provides for the duration of copyright. The Act also provides for the registration of works under Section 45, although registration is not mandatory.

Procedure

While the procedure for dealing with copyright cases in India is largely governed by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908, and the Specific Relief Act, 1963, the Copyright Act, 1957, provides for certain special procedures [3]. For example, Section 55 of the Act provides for the seizure of infringing copies, while Section 58 provides for the disposal of seized copies. The Act also provides for the appointment of a Copyright Board under Section 11, which has the power to hear and decide cases relating to copyright.

Key Cases

  • Eastern Book Company v. Navin J. Desai (2001) - This case established that copyright infringement is a tort and can be sued upon as such [4].
  • Bharat Glass Works v. Gopal Glass Works (1999) - This case held that the design of a functional article is not eligible for copyright protection [5].
  • R.G. Anand v. M/s. Delux Films (1978) - This case established that plagiarism is a form of copyright infringement and can be actionable [6].
