GI Tag Registration Procedure in India

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

The Geographical Indications (GI) tag is a sign used on products that have a specific geographical origin and possess qualities or a reputation that are due to that origin. In India, the GI tag is granted by the Geographical Indications Registry, which is headquartered in Chennai. The registration of a GI tag provides legal protection to the product and prevents unauthorized use by others.

Overview

The GI tag registration procedure in India is governed by the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [1]. The Act provides a framework for the registration and protection of GIs in India. The registration of a GI tag is essential to protect the interests of the producers and consumers of the product. It also helps to promote the product and increase its market value. The GI tag is granted for a period of 10 years, which can be renewed for a further period of 10 years.

Legal Framework

The GI tag registration procedure in India is governed by the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, and the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Rules, 2002 [2]. According to Section 11 of the Act, any association of persons or producers or any organization or authority established by or under any law for the time being in force representing the interest of the producers of the concerned goods can apply for the registration of a GI tag. The application for registration of a GI tag must be made in the prescribed form and must be accompanied by the prescribed fee [3].

Procedure

The procedure for GI tag registration in India involves several steps. The first step is to conduct a search of the existing GIs to ensure that the proposed GI does not conflict with any existing GI. The next step is to prepare and file the application for registration of the GI tag. The application must be made in the prescribed form and must be accompanied by the prescribed fee. The application must also be accompanied by a statement of the case, a map, and a specimen of the GI tag. After the application is filed, it is examined by the Geographical Indications Registry to determine whether it meets the requirements of the Act and the Rules. If the application is accepted, the GI tag is registered and a certificate of registration is issued to the applicant.

Key Cases

Some landmark cases related to GI tag registration in India include the Darjeeling Tea case [4], where the Calcutta High Court held that the GI tag for Darjeeling tea could be granted to tea producers in the Darjeeling district of West Bengal. Another significant case is the Basmati Rice case [5], where the Delhi High Court held that the GI tag for Basmati rice could be granted to rice producers in certain regions of India. The Mysore Silk case [6] is also notable, where the Karnataka High Court held that the GI tag for Mysore silk could be granted to silk producers in the Mysore region of Karnataka.
