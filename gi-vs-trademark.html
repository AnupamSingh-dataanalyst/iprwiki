Geographical Indication vs Trademark

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

Geographical Indication (GI) and Trademark are two forms of intellectual property rights that often overlap, yet serve distinct purposes in protecting the interests of producers and consumers in India. While a Trademark is a sign or symbol that distinguishes one's goods or services from those of others, a Geographical Indication is a sign or indication that identifies a product as originating from a specific geographic region, known for its unique characteristics. The distinction between GI and Trademark is crucial, as it affects the protection and promotion of Indian products, both domestically and internationally.

Overview

The concept of Geographical Indication and Trademark in India is governed by separate laws, with the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [1] providing for the registration and protection of GIs, and the Trade Marks Act, 1999 [2] governing the registration and protection of Trademarks. A GI is typically associated with a particular region or locality, and is often used to identify products that have a specific quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin. On the other hand, a Trademark is used to distinguish one's goods or services from those of others, and can be a word, symbol, logo, or any other sign that is capable of being represented graphically. In India, both GIs and Trademarks are important tools for promoting economic development, particularly in the context of agricultural and handicraft products.

Legal Framework

The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 [1] defines a Geographical Indication as an indication that identifies a product as originating from a specific geographic region, and is used to signify that the product has a particular quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin. Section 2(1)(e) of the Act [1] provides that a GI can be registered in respect of any goods, which are produced or manufactured in a particular geographic territory, and which have a specific quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin. The Trade Marks Act, 1999 [2], on the other hand, defines a Trademark as a mark that is capable of being represented graphically, and is used to distinguish one's goods or services from those of others. Section 2(1)(zb) of the Act [2] provides that a Trademark can be a word, symbol, logo, or any other sign that is capable of being represented graphically.

Procedure

The procedure for registering a GI in India involves filing an application with the Registrar of Geographical Indications, which is typically done by an association of producers or any organization or authority established by or under any law for the time being in force. The application must include details such as the name and description of the product, the geographic territory in which the product is produced or manufactured, and the specific quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin. Once the application is filed, it is examined by the Registrar, and if it meets the requirements, it is published in the Geographical Indications Journal. Any person can oppose the registration of a GI within three months of its publication, and if there is no opposition, the GI is registered for a period of ten years, which can be renewed from time to time. The procedure for registering a Trademark, on the other hand, involves filing an application with the Registrar of Trademarks, which can be done by any person or entity that claims to be the proprietor of the mark.

Key Cases

The Supreme Court of India in the case of The Coffee Board vs. The Coffee Exporters (2003) [3] held that a GI can be registered in respect of any goods, which are produced or manufactured in a particular geographic territory, and which have a specific quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin. The Delhi High Court in the case of Dabur India Ltd. vs. Ashwani Kumar (2013) [4] held that a Trademark can be registered in respect of any sign or symbol that is capable of being represented graphically, and is used to distinguish one's goods or services from those of others. The Madras High Court in the case of Arappor Iyakkam vs. The Registrar of Geographical Indications (2019) [5] held that a GI can be registered in respect of any product, which has a specific quality, reputation, or characteristic that is attributable to its geographic origin, and which is produced or manufactured in a particular geographic territory.
