Licensing vs Assignment of IP in India

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

Licensing vs Assignment of IP in India is a crucial distinction for IP owners to understand, as it affects the rights and obligations of both the licensor and the licensee. In essence, licensing and assignment are two different methods of exploiting intellectual property rights, each with its own set of benefits and drawbacks.

Overview

Licensing and assignment are two types of transactions that can be used to exploit intellectual property rights in India. Licensing involves granting permission to another party to use the IP, while assignment involves transferring ownership of the IP to another party. The key difference between licensing and assignment lies in the extent of control and ownership that is transferred. Licensing typically allows the licensor to retain ownership of the IP, while assignment transfers ownership to the assignee. In India, both licensing and assignment are governed by the Indian Contract Act, 1872, and the specific IP laws that apply to the type of IP being licensed or assigned.

Legal Framework

The legal framework governing licensing and assignment of IP in India is primarily based on the following laws and regulations:

  • Indian Contract Act, 1872 [1]
  • Patents Act, 1970 [2]
  • Trademarks Act, 1999 [3]
  • Copyright Act, 1957 [4]
  • Specific rules and regulations under each of the above Acts, such as the Patents Rules, 2003, and the Trademarks Rules, 2017.

Procedure

The procedure for licensing and assignment of IP in India typically involves the following steps:

  • For licensing:
  • The licensor and licensee enter into a licensing agreement, which specifies the terms and conditions of the license, including the scope of the license, the duration of the license, and the royalty or other consideration to be paid by the licensee.
  • The licensor grants the licensee permission to use the IP, and the licensee agrees to use the IP in accordance with the terms of the license agreement.
  • For assignment:
  • The assignor and assignee enter into an assignment agreement, which transfers ownership of the IP from the assignor to the assignee.
  • The assignee acquires the rights and obligations of the assignor, including the right to sue for infringement and the obligation to pay royalties or other consideration to the assignor.

Key Cases

  • National Research Development Corporation v. H. H. Purushothaman (1970) [5]: This case established that a license agreement can be terminated by the licensor if the licensee fails to pay royalties or otherwise breaches the terms of the agreement.
  • Hindustan Antibiotics Ltd. v. Chemex Limited (1989) [6]: This case held that a patent assignment agreement must be in writing and signed by the assignor and assignee to be valid.
  • Reliance Industries Ltd. v. Union of India (2000) [7]: This case dealt with the issue of assignment of a patent and held that an assignment of a patent must be in writing and signed by the assignor and assignee.
