Design Infringement in India

AI-generated  ·  iPgraam IPR Knowledge Base  ·  Indian IP Law Reference

Design infringement in India refers to the unauthorized use of a registered design, which can lead to legal consequences for the infringer. The Designs Act, 2000, governs design infringement in India, providing a framework for protecting original and unique designs. In India, design infringement can occur in various industries, including textiles, furniture, and consumer products, and can result in significant financial losses for the design owner.

Overview

Design infringement in India is a serious issue, as it can undermine the creative efforts of designers and manufacturers who invest time and resources in developing original designs. The Designs Act, 2000, defines a design as "the features of shape, configuration, pattern, ornamentation, or composition of lines or colours applied to any article, whether in two dimensional or three dimensional, or in both forms, by any industrial process or means, whether manual, mechanical or chemical, separate or combined, which in the finished article appeal to and are judged solely by the eye." [1] The Act provides a framework for registering designs, which can help prevent design infringement. In India, design registration is essential to protect designs from infringement, as it provides the owner with exclusive rights to use the design.

Legal Framework

The Designs Act, 2000, is the primary legislation governing design infringement in India. Section 22 of the Act states that the registration of a design gives the proprietor a monopoly for a period of ten years from the date of registration. Section 23 of the Act provides that the proprietor of a registered design has the exclusive right to apply the design to any article in any class in which the design is registered. Section 11 of the Act defines design infringement as the use of a registered design or a design which is substantially similar to a registered design, without the permission of the proprietor. The Act also provides for penalties for design infringement, including fines and imprisonment.

Procedure

To prevent design infringement, designers and manufacturers can register their designs with the Design Wing of the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks. The registration process involves filing an application with the Design Office, which includes a representation of the design, a statement of the novelty of the design, and a fee. Once the application is filed, it is examined by the Design Office to determine whether the design is new and original. If the design is registered, the proprietor has exclusive rights to use the design for a period of ten years from the date of registration. In case of design infringement, the proprietor can file a suit in a court of law, seeking damages and an injunction to stop the infringing activity.

Key Cases

  • Milcent Fibres Ltd. vs. Union of India & Ors. (1994): The Delhi High Court held that a design registration can be cancelled if the design is not new or original.
  • Carlsberg Breweries vs. Som Distilleries & Breweries Ltd. (2004): The Delhi High Court held that a design infringement action can be maintained even if the registered design is not being used by the proprietor.
  • Jyoti Ltd. vs. Star Wire (India) Ltd. (1994): The Bombay High Court held that a design infringement action requires proof of actual use of the registered design by the proprietor.
